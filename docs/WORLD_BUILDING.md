# Worldbuilding and Lore

## Setting

The game takes place on an unnamed, unknown planet. It is not Earth, though some life is familiar in broad form: the world has organic plant life and a humanoid species. Keep its ecology and history distinct from Earth's. The planet's name, regions, and exact biology remain open.

## The buried civilization

Long before the story, this planet held a thriving society with advanced technology and a highly developed public life. Something caused that civilization to collapse. The cause is deliberately unresolved; it should remain a source of mystery until the story has a reason to answer it.

Over time, a harsher, more organic world took hold across the old civilization's remains. The former city is not an untouched ruin: roots and vines split its surfaces, plants grow through machines and buildings, and weather has worn down exposed structures. New life lives among, on top of, and inside the remnants of the old world.

## Story opening

The story begins when a human pilot crash-lands on the planet. The pilot meets the Brute, an alien local who has survived on the surface and knows this living world firsthand. As they explore, they eventually find the Tinkerer: an old, broken-down robot left over from the collapsed society.

The Tinkerer connects the present world to the civilization beneath it, but its age and damaged condition mean it need not know or explain the whole truth. The pilot, Brute, and Tinkerer each encounter the planet from a different vantage point: newcomer, native, and remnant.

## Visual direction

The central material contrast is advanced manufacture being reclaimed by organic life and weather:

- Chrome and other once-polished metals are dulled, scratched, oxidized, or rusted.
- Vines, roots, leaves, and other plant growth weave over and through the old city.
- Surfaces show long exposure to weather: staining, erosion, cracked finishes, debris, and softened edges.
- Old technology should still read as purposeful and sophisticated beneath its damage; it was not primitive when built.
- The surface world should feel vigorous and adapted, rather than like decoration placed on an otherwise empty ruin.

Use this contrast to guide environments, props, character materials, and color choices. Avoid making every location equally overgrown or every old machine equally broken; intact, exposed, and newly reclaimed areas can reveal different parts of the world's history.

## Story and design principles

- Let environmental details show the relationship between the vanished society and the life that followed it.
- Keep the cause of the collapse open until a deliberate story decision is made.
- Distinguish the pilot's unfamiliarity, the Brute's local knowledge, and the Tinker's partial connection to the past through what each can notice or interpret.
- Treat the Tinkerer as a character, not merely a lore terminal; damaged memory or limited perspective can preserve mystery.
- Let the planet's organic life have its own forms and behavior rather than treating it as Earth vegetation copied onto ruins.

## Open questions

- What caused the advanced society to collapse, and should the player ever learn the full answer?
- Who were the people of that society, and how does the Brute's species relate to them?
- What brought the human pilot to this planet, and is the crash accidental or connected to the larger story?
- What can the Tinkerer remember, and what has it misunderstood or lost?
- What are the planet, its regions, and its distinctive forms of life called?
